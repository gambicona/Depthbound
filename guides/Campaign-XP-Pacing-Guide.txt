Depthbound Campaign XP Pacing Guide
===================================

Purpose
-------

Use this guide when planning full campaign arcs or mini-campaigns made from
multiple dungeons.

This is not a story guide. It is a practical pacing guide for:

- how many dungeons a campaign should contain
- how many monsters to place
- which monster categories to use
- how much optional overfill is healthy
- how to avoid accidentally overleveling a party

The assumed party is 4 heroes starting at 0 XP.


Core Pacing Idea
----------------

Depthbound dungeons should be built with optional overfill.

That means a dungeon can contain more XP than the party is expected to earn,
because players normally should not clear every room, every branch, every
optional route, and every hidden boss.

Healthy default:

- Full-clear dungeon XP: about 130-150% of the intended level progress.
- Normal-play XP: about 65-75% of the placed dungeon XP.
- Completionist parties may level faster, and that is acceptable.

This matches the Barrow Crown model:

- Full-clear Barrow Crown currently ends near level 9.
- A normal partial-clear run around 65-75% ends closer to level 8.
- This is healthy if optional rooms, alternate routes, and avoidable encounters
  are expected.


Baseline Dungeon Sizes
----------------------

For a 4-hero party:

Small dungeon:
- 12-18 monsters placed
- 8-12 monsters expected to be fought
- 1 boss
- Good for side dungeons or short campaign chapters

Standard dungeon:
- 20-30 monsters placed
- 14-21 monsters expected to be fought
- 1-2 bosses
- Good for most main campaign dungeons

Large dungeon:
- 32-45 monsters placed
- 22-32 monsters expected to be fought
- 2-3 bosses
- Good for major chapter dungeons

Finale dungeon:
- 35-55 monsters placed
- 24-38 monsters expected to be fought
- 2-4 bosses
- Good for end-of-arc or end-of-campaign dungeons


Category By Party Level
-----------------------

Use the dungeon category as the main danger band.

Levels 1-2:
- Main category: Cat 1
- Sprinkle in: a few Cat 2 elites
- Avoid too much: Cat 2 bosses

Levels 3-4:
- Main category: Cat 2
- Sprinkle in: Cat 1 mobs, rare Cat 3 elite
- Avoid too much: multiple Cat 3 bosses

Levels 5-6:
- Main category: Cat 3
- Sprinkle in: Cat 2 support, rare Cat 4 elite
- Avoid too much: Cat 4 boss chains

Levels 7-8:
- Main category: Cat 4
- Sprinkle in: Cat 3 mobs, rare Cat 5 elite
- Avoid too much: too many Cat 5 monsters

Levels 9-10:
- Main category: Cat 5
- Sprinkle in: Cat 4 support, rare Cat 6 elite
- Avoid too much: Cat 6 boss spam

Levels 11-12:
- Main category: Cat 6
- Sprinkle in: Cat 5 support, rare Cat 7 elite
- Avoid too much: repeated next-category bosses

Levels 13-14:
- Main category: Cat 7
- Sprinkle in: Cat 6 support, rare Cat 8 elite
- Avoid too much: repeated next-category bosses

Levels 15-16:
- Main category: Cat 8
- Sprinkle in: Cat 7 support, rare Cat 9 elite
- Avoid too much: repeated next-category bosses

Levels 17-18:
- Main category: Cat 9
- Sprinkle in: Cat 8 support, rare Cat 10 elite
- Avoid too much: repeated next-category bosses

Levels 19-20:
- Main category: Cat 10
- Sprinkle in: Cat 9 support
- Avoid too much: extra boss density unless the campaign is ending


Healthy Monster Mix Per Dungeon
-------------------------------

For a standard dungeon, a good default mix is:

- 70% normal monsters from the dungeon category
- 15% weaker monsters from the previous category
- 10% elite monsters from the current or next category
- 5% bosses or unique monsters

Example Cat 3 dungeon with 30 monsters placed:

- 21 Cat 3 normal monsters
- 4-5 Cat 2 weaker monsters
- 3 Cat 3 elites or Cat 4 elites
- 1-2 bosses

Boss count rule:

- Small dungeon: 1 boss
- Standard dungeon: 1 main boss, maybe 1 optional boss
- Large dungeon: 1 main boss plus 1-2 optional/minibosses
- Finale dungeon: 1 final boss plus 2-3 major optional bosses

The main pacing danger is not total monster count. It is boss XP density.
Several boss-tagged monsters in the same dungeon can jump the party ahead very
quickly.


Campaign Option A: Classic Full Campaign
----------------------------------------

Use this for a full level 1-20 campaign with steady pacing.

Total length:
- 19-20 dungeons

Arc 1:
- Levels: 1-4
- Dungeons: 4
- Categories: Cat 1, then Cat 2

Arc 2:
- Levels: 5-8
- Dungeons: 4
- Categories: Cat 3-4

Arc 3:
- Levels: 9-12
- Dungeons: 4
- Categories: Cat 5-6

Arc 4:
- Levels: 13-16
- Dungeons: 4
- Categories: Cat 7-8

Arc 5:
- Levels: 17-20
- Dungeons: 3-4
- Categories: Cat 9-10

Typical dungeon:
- 20-35 monsters placed
- 1-2 bosses
- 2-5 elite or rare enemies
- 65-75% expected clear rate


Campaign Option B: Compact Campaign
-----------------------------------

Use this for a punchier level 1-20 campaign with larger dungeons and bigger
chapter jumps.

Total length:
- 14 dungeons

Arc 1:
- Levels: 1-5
- Dungeons: 3
- Categories: Cat 1-2

Arc 2:
- Levels: 6-10
- Dungeons: 4
- Categories: Cat 3-5

Arc 3:
- Levels: 11-15
- Dungeons: 4
- Categories: Cat 6-8

Arc 4:
- Levels: 16-20
- Dungeons: 3
- Categories: Cat 8-10

Typical dungeon:
- 30-45 monsters placed
- 2-3 bosses
- multiple optional wings
- 60-70% expected clear rate


Campaign Option C: Barrow Crown Style Mini-Campaign
---------------------------------------------------

Use this for one named campaign arc.

Total length:
- 7 dungeons

Expected result:
- Starts at level 1 with 0 XP
- Ends around level 8 on normal partial-clear play
- May end around level 9 on full completionist play

Dungeon 1:
- Intended level after: 2
- Category mix: Cat 1, a little Cat 2
- Monsters placed: 18-24

Dungeon 2:
- Intended level after: 3
- Category mix: Cat 1-2
- Monsters placed: 22-30

Dungeon 3:
- Intended level after: 4
- Category mix: Cat 2
- Monsters placed: 24-34

Dungeon 4:
- Intended level after: 5
- Category mix: Cat 2, a little Cat 3
- Monsters placed: 26-36

Dungeon 5:
- Intended level after: 6
- Category mix: Cat 3
- Monsters placed: 26-38

Dungeon 6:
- Intended level after: 7
- Category mix: Cat 3-4
- Monsters placed: 30-42

Dungeon 7:
- Intended level after: 8
- Category mix: Cat 4 finale, rare Cat 5
- Monsters placed: 32-48

This is a good default model for future named arcs.


Campaign Option D: Three-Act Campaign
-------------------------------------

Use this when a campaign should feel complete without becoming a very long
sequence of separate arcs.

Total length:
- 17 dungeons

Act 1:
- Levels: 1-6
- Dungeons: 5
- Feel: low-tier discovery

Act 2:
- Levels: 7-13
- Dungeons: 6
- Feel: dangerous midgame

Act 3:
- Levels: 14-20
- Dungeons: 6
- Feel: high-tier war/endgame

Per act:
- First dungeon: easier, mostly current category
- Middle dungeons: standard category
- Final dungeon: current category plus next-category elites or bosses


Recommended Workflow For New Campaigns
--------------------------------------

1. Pick the campaign structure first.
   Good defaults:
   - 7 dungeons for a named mini-campaign
   - 14 dungeons for a compact full campaign
   - 17-20 dungeons for a full long campaign

2. Decide the expected final level.
   Do this before writing rooms or placing monsters.

3. Assign one intended level gain per dungeon unless using a compact campaign.

4. Place more XP than required.
   A good target is 130-150% of the XP needed for the intended level progress.

5. Make 25-35% of the dungeon optional.
   Optional content can be:
   - side rooms
   - alternate wings
   - hidden bosses
   - locked treasure routes
   - dangerous shortcuts
   - faction-specific branches

6. Keep boss density under control.
   Boss-tagged monsters should feel meaningful. If a dungeon has many named
   enemies, consider making some of them elite normal monsters or giving them
   campaign-specific XP overrides.

7. Simulate the full-clear version.
   Full-clear should usually end 1 level ahead at most.

8. Simulate a 65-75% clear version.
   This should match the intended campaign progression.


Quick Rule Of Thumb
-------------------

If the campaign is built like Barrow Crown, use this rule:

- 7 dungeons should cover about 7 levels of progress.
- Full-clear may overshoot by about 1 level.
- Normal partial-clear should land on the intended final level.
- Completionist overleveling is okay as long as it does not break the next arc.

For future mini-campaigns, Barrow Crown is a healthy reference:

- dense dungeons
- optional routes expected
- some missed content assumed
- full-clear parties rewarded with faster progression
- normal parties still follow the intended curve
