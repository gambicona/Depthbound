Monster Encounter Showcase Log

Purpose

This is a lookup sheet for interesting monster groups that show off AI behavior,
monster roles, and dungeon-theme identity. These are not strict balance packs;
tune monster counts, categories, and room size to the party level.

Use this when you want an encounter to feel like a small tactical scene instead
of a pile of enemies.


Old Guardroom

1. Hollow Choir Command Post
   Monsters: Bone-Priest of the Hollow Choir (C6), Adamant-Bone Sentinel (C7), Tomb-Star Arbalest (C9)
   Type: Boss/support plus normal monsters
   What is cool:
   The Sentinel gives the party a clear front line problem, the Arbalest punishes
   exposed heroes, and the Bone-Priest has meaningful choices between pressure,
   support, and punishment. This is a good showcase for the special-action scorer:
   heal/support if the line is damaged, attack/control if the party is clustered
   or vulnerable.

2. Rusted Guard Formation
   Monsters: Marrow Standard Bearer (C7), Black-Bone Halberdier (C6), Ossuary Spellblade (C6)
   Type: Non-boss tactical squad
   What is cool:
   This feels like trained undead soldiers rather than random skeletons. The
   Halberdiers create reach and movement pressure, the Spellblade can threaten
   softer targets, and the Standard Bearer makes the group read as coordinated.
   Good for testing whether players respect monster zones of control.

3. Corpse-Tide Breakthrough
   Monsters: Corpse-Tide Colossus (C8), Grave Ghoul (C1), Shambling Corpse (C1) or Bloated Rotter (C1)
   Type: Big brute plus bodies
   What is cool:
   The Colossus is the obvious danger, but the weaker undead clog lanes and make
   target selection matter. This is useful when you want a slower encounter where
   the drama comes from the party trying to stop the large undead before the room
   collapses into a scrum.

4. Haunted Barracks
   Monsters: Warden of the Old Watch (C2), Ruin Arbalester (C3), Rusted Shieldbearer (C1)
   Type: Theme-forward old-guardroom patrol
   What is cool:
   The Shieldbearers make a stubborn front, the Arbalesters fire from behind, and
   the Warden gives the encounter a named tactical center. It is readable for the
   player: break the shield wall, dive the archers, or burn down the leader.


Forest Of The Beasts

1. Grove Bodyguard Cell
   Monsters: Ironbark Guardian (C5), Sparkwing Mephitling (C1), Myconid Sporeguard (C3)
   Type: Guardian plus ranged ally plus healer
   What is cool:
   This is one of the cleanest behavior showcases. The Guardian holds the line,
   the Sparkwing tries to shoot from a safer distance, and the Sporeguard can heal
   wounded allies. The party gets a real priority puzzle: kill the healer, chase
   the kiter, or break the wall.

2. Pack And Charge
   Monsters: Forest Wolf (C1) or Moonfang Dire Wolf (C5), Bramble Boar (C1) or Elder Briarboar (C4)
   Type: Non-boss beast encounter
   What is cool:
   Wolves are good for swarm/focus behavior, while the boar gives the encounter
   a charger that wants to hit from distance. It creates a fast, physical fight
   where spacing and protecting wounded heroes matter.

3. Fungal Nursery
   Monsters: Fungal Broodmother (C3), Myconid Sporeguard (C3), Violet Fungus Cluster (C1) or Shrieker Sporecap (C2)
   Type: Support/control ecology encounter
   What is cool:
   The Broodmother is the pressure piece, the Sporeguard is the sustain piece,
   and the smaller fungi make the room feel occupied and hazardous. This is a
   good encounter for showing that "plant/fungus" does not just mean slow melee.

4. First Forest Court
   Monsters: Root of the First Forest (C10), Ironbark Guardian (C5), Needlewind Skirmisher (C2)
   Type: Boss plus protector plus mobile attacker
   What is cool:
   The boss feels like the ancient heart of the room, the Guardian buys it time,
   and the Skirmisher punishes wounded or isolated characters. This is a good
   late-forest encounter because the monsters create different problems at once.


Underdark Depths

1. Shrieking Mycelium Den
   Monsters: Hookcap Mycelial Horror (C4), Myconid Sporeguard (C3), Shrieker Sporecap (C2)
   Type: Non-boss fungus encounter
   What is cool:
   The Shrieker provides alarm/control flavor, the Sporeguard can heal or support,
   and the Horror becomes the actual thing the party must stop. The encounter
   reads like a living cave defense system.

2. Webbed Beetle Ambush
   Monsters: Pale Cave Spider (C1), Nightglass Spider (C3), Razor-Mandible Beetle (C2)
   Type: Non-boss predator mix
   What is cool:
   Spiders give skirmish and ambush pressure, while the beetle supplies blunt
   melee threat. This is a good compact encounter when you want the underdark to
   feel predatory without using a boss.

3. Deep Mycelial Throne
   Monsters: Deep Mycelial Overmind (C10), Fungal Broodmother (C3), Spore Titan of the Deep (C7)
   Type: Boss/control fungus court
   What is cool:
   This is a heavy support/control fight. The player should feel that the fungi
   are sharing a mind: healing, pressure, and control all compete for the monsters'
   best action. Good for testing high-level special choice.

4. Pressure Cave Collapse
   Monsters: Stone-Crown Warden (C4), Mudslide Ravager (C4), Pressure Rift Horror (C4)
   Type: Elemental cave encounter
   What is cool:
   Stone holds the line, mud disrupts positioning, and pressure magic makes the
   room feel unstable. This is a strong underdark alternative to beasts or fungi.


Desert Ruins

1. Sun-Baked Ambush
   Monsters: Glass-Fang Viper (C2), Dust Hyena (C1), Sand Scorpion (C1)
   Type: Non-boss desert predator pack
   What is cool:
   Hyenas create pack pressure, the Viper threatens a priority target, and the
   Scorpion gives a tougher control/brute angle. It is quick, readable, and nasty.

2. Haunted Reliquary
   Monsters: Whispering Shade (C1), Candleflame Wisp (C1), Grave-Lantern Specter (C1)
   Type: Non-boss ghost encounter
   What is cool:
   This feels like the ruin's memory fighting the party. The ghosts are fragile
   enough to handle, but their magic and target choices make the encounter feel
   less like a normal melee brawl.

3. Glasswind Killing Ground
   Monsters: Glasswind Assassin (C5), Sirocco Reaver (C3), Sandstorm Burrower (C5)
   Type: Mobile elemental/desert encounter
   What is cool:
   This is a movement showcase. The enemies reposition, pressure angles, and make
   the battlefield feel windy and unstable. Excellent for testing opportunity
   attacks, kiting, and target chasing.

4. Dune Guardian Procession
   Monsters: Duneplate Tortoise (C5) or Ancient Glassback Tortoise (C3), Bonecrack Scorpion (C3), Sirocco Wyvern (C7)
   Type: Boss-like beast mix without a true boss
   What is cool:
   The tortoise anchors the encounter, the scorpion punishes careless melee, and
   the wyvern creates a mobile threat. It feels like desert wildlife guarding an
   ancient road or sealed ruin.


Embervein Deepworks

1. Foundry Firing Line
   Monsters: Emberstorm Artillery (C5), Cinder Lemure (C1) or Kindled Ash Brute (C1), Chain-Snag Devil (C2)
   Type: Ranged artillery plus screen plus controller
   What is cool:
   This is a very clean AI showcase. The screen tries to keep the party occupied,
   the Artillery seeks useful shots, and the Chain-Snag Devil adds control. The
   player sees the problem immediately: get past the front or suffer ranged fire.

2. Furnace Maintenance Crew
   Monsters: Furnace-Core Myrmidon (C3), Obsidian Smelter (C4), Sootflame Taskmaster (C2)
   Type: Non-boss industrial fire team
   What is cool:
   These monsters feel like the dungeon machinery has become hostile. The Myrmidon
   and Smelter bring heavy fire/forge pressure, while the Taskmaster gives the
   encounter a command-unit flavor.

3. Bad Air In The Mine
   Monsters: Pressure Rift Horror (C4), Dust Devil (C1), Smoke Mephit Sneak (C2)
   Type: Control and movement encounter
   What is cool:
   Not every Embervein fight needs to be pure fire. This one is about bad air,
   pressure shifts, and sneaky movement. It is useful for rooms with vents, shafts,
   or unstable tunnels.

4. Heart Of The Deepworks
   Monsters: The Embervein, Awakened (C10), Hell-Crown Praetor (C9), Furnace Tyrant (C7) or Furnace-Core Myrmidon (C3)
   Type: Boss plus commander plus brute
   What is cool:
   The Awakened is the dungeon heart, the Praetor adds infernal command/support,
   and the brute prevents the party from casually rushing the boss. This should
   feel like the whole industrial hell-engine waking up.


Depths Of Hells

1. Chain Court
   Monsters: Hell-Crown Praetor (C9), Infernal Gaoler (C2), Chain-Snag Devil (C2)
   Type: Devil commander plus control squad
   What is cool:
   This is organized cruelty. Chains control movement, the Gaoler punishes trapped
   targets, and the Praetor can support or punish depending on the turn. This is
   a good encounter for showing devils as disciplined tactical enemies.

2. Contract Office From Hell
   Monsters: Soul-Ledger Scribe (C5), Contract Whelp (C1), Spined Hellion (C2)
   Type: Non-boss infernal bureaucracy encounter
   What is cool:
   The Scribe feels like the brain of the encounter, the Whelps create nuisance
   pressure, and the ranged devil punishes characters who ignore the backline.
   Funny premise, real tactical teeth.

3. Demon Breach
   Monsters: Blood-Smoke Howler (C2), Blink-Rift Predator (C5), Claw Imp of Ruin (C1)
   Type: Chaotic demon pack
   What is cool:
   This should contrast strongly with devil fights. It is fast, violent, and messy:
   the Predator threatens weak positions, the Howler adds pressure, and the Imps
   make the party spend actions cleaning up chaos.

4. Hellchain Tribunal
   Monsters: Hellchain Exarch (C7), Chain Magistrate (C4), Furnace Tyrant (C7)
   Type: Boss devil chain encounter
   What is cool:
   This is a late-theme chain-control fight. The party has to decide whether to
   kill the controller, survive the brute, or spend resources breaking the enemy
   formation. It should feel oppressive but understandable.


Crucible Of Flame

1. Ember Battery
   Monsters: Emberstorm Artillery (C5), Cinder Lemure (C1), Smoke Mephit Sneak (C2)
   Type: Non-boss artillery screen
   What is cool:
   The Lemures screen, the Artillery blasts, and the Sneak pressures heroes who
   try to solve the fight by standing still. A very good "basic fire tactics"
   encounter.

2. Smoke And Magma
   Monsters: Magma Myrmidon (C5), Smoke-Veil Assassin (C5), Ash Paraelemental Strangler (C3)
   Type: Mixed control and damage
   What is cool:
   Smoke makes targeting and pursuit awkward, magma creates heavy pressure, and
   the Strangler adds control. This fight should make the player care about both
   position and priority targets.

3. Caldera Gate
   Monsters: Warden of the Caldera Gate (C8), Lava-Tide Colossus (C6), Ember Wisp (C1) as adds
   Type: Boss plus huge brute
   What is cool:
   One monster is the ritual/terrain threat, the other is the physical disaster.
   Wisps let the room feel alive without stealing the spotlight.


Crucible Of Stone

1. Living Wall
   Monsters: Stone-Crown Warden (C4), Graniteback Bear (C3 or high-tier C6) or Basalt Mauler (C3), Crystal-Carapace Scarab (C3)
   Type: Defensive stone encounter
   What is cool:
   This is a durable formation. The Warden can show off regrowth/healing-style
   support when appropriate, while the other monsters make a stubborn front line.

2. Mudslide Ambush
   Monsters: Mudslide Ravager (C4), Sandstorm Burrower (C5), Boulder Boar (C2)
   Type: Mobile earth encounter
   What is cool:
   Earth does not have to mean slow. The Burrower and Boar create movement spikes,
   while the Myrmidon disrupts the party's preferred formation.

3. Idol Chamber
   Monsters: Graven Idol Guardian (C2) or Stone-Crown Warden (C4), Pebbleling (C1) or Dust Mephitling (C1), Stoneback Beetle (C1)
   Type: Boss-like temple defense
   What is cool:
   This is a good stone-crucible centerpiece: a hard central guardian with smaller
   creatures that make action economy matter. The party must decide whether to
   clear bodies or commit damage to the idol/warden.


Crucible Of Storms

1. Needle Draft Squadron
   Monsters: Needlewind Skirmisher (C2), Sparkwing Mephitling (C1), Thunderclap Sprite (C2)
   Type: Non-boss mobile air team
   What is cool:
   This is perfect for testing skirmisher and kiter behavior. The Skirmisher wants
   wounded targets, the Sparkwing wants distance, and the Sprite adds burst/control
   flavor. Opportunity attacks and reactions matter here.

2. Pressure Front
   Monsters: Pressure Rift Horror (C4), Dust Devil (C1), Sirocco Reaver (C3)
   Type: Movement/control storm encounter
   What is cool:
   The room should feel hard to stand in. The monsters pull, slip, pressure, and
   chase in different ways, forcing the party to adapt rather than form a static
   line.

3. Storm Throne
   Monsters: Sky-Crown Stormlord (C5), Storm-Shell Core (C2), Needlewind Skirmisher (C2)
   Type: Boss plus defensive core plus mobile attackers
   What is cool:
   The Stormlord is spectacle, the Core gives support/defense texture, and the
   Skirmishers keep the fight from becoming a stationary boss race.


Crucible Of Tides

1. Mending Current Cell
   Monsters: Whitewater Matron (C3), Tide Mauler (C3) or Tidepool Tyrant (C2), Reefjaw Shark (C2)
   Type: Healer plus bruiser plus predator
   What is cool:
   This is a clear healer showcase. The Matron gives the party a support target,
   the bruiser holds attention, and the shark-style predator punishes wounded or
   exposed heroes.

2. Steam And Mist Oracle
   Monsters: Steamstorm Oracle (C6), Fogbank Harrier (C2), River Myrmidon (C3) or Steam Mephitling (C1)
   Type: Caster/control water encounter
   What is cool:
   The Oracle gives magical pressure, the Harrier repositions, and the brute makes
   chasing the backline costly. Good for a room with vents, pools, or mist cover.

3. Blackwater Wall
   Monsters: Blackwater Colossus (C6), Glacierback Guardian (C5), Shock Eel (C2) or Storm Eel (C3)
   Type: Heavy water boss-style fight
   What is cool:
   The Colossus is the big offensive problem, the Guardian is the anchor, and the
   eel creates a dangerous side threat. Simple, readable, and dramatic.

4. Maelstrom Court
   Monsters: Maelstrom Baron (C7), Whitewater Matron (C3), Dripling (C1) or Steam Mephitling (C1)
   Type: Boss plus healer plus adds
   What is cool:
   The Baron creates the main threat, the Matron makes target priority matter, and
   the lesser elementals make the battlefield crowded enough for the action scorer
   to choose between healing, offense, and fallback attacks.


General Encounter Design Notes

- Guardian plus ranged ally is one of the clearest player-readable setups.
- Healer plus brute works best when the healer is reachable but not free.
- Skirmishers and kiters need enough room to move, or they become normal melee.
- Artillery needs either distance, a screen, or both.
- Swarm monsters are most interesting when they can choose the same wounded target.
- Controllers are strongest when the room has lanes, doors, or hazards to exploit.
- Bosses feel smarter when paired with one monster that protects them and one that
  pressures the party's backline.
- Non-boss encounters are usually better with two clear roles than with five mixed
  roles. Let the player understand the problem quickly.
