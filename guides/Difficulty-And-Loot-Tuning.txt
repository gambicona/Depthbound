How to tune dungeon difficulty and loot generation

This file describes the current practical knobs. Most are in src/scripts/app.js unless noted otherwise.

Dungeon size

createInitialState uses the selected theme's generator settings.

Theme files:
src/scripts/content/themes/old-guardroom.js
src/scripts/content/themes/forest-of-the-beasts.js

Useful theme fields:

generator.roomCount
generator.roomCountByCategory

More rooms means more chances for monsters, chests, furniture, traps, and rests to matter.

Monster categories

categoryForHeroLevel(level) currently maps:
levels 1-2 -> category 1
levels 3-4 -> category 2
levels 5-6 -> category 3

Monster templates define category or cat. A monster can spawn if its category is not above the party's current category.

Theme monster pools

Themes use monsterTagGroups and bossMonsterTagGroups.

Example:

monsterTagGroups: [
  ["undead", "old-guardroom"],
]

The monster must match one full tag group to be eligible.

Room monster chance

In createDungeonMonsters:

if (room.id === bossRoomId || Math.random() >= 0.72) continue;

The 0.72 means about 28% of rooms get a monster group. Lower the number for more fights, raise it for fewer fights.

Monster count per room

roomMonsterSpawnTuning controls how many monsters are in one monster room:

roomMonsterSpawnTuning = {
  baseCount: 1,
  extraPerPartyMember: 0.65,
  categoryGapBonus: 0.25,
  maximumCount: 5,
}

baseCount: minimum count for a monster room.
extraPerPartyMember: extra expected monsters for each party member beyond one.
categoryGapBonus: extra expected monsters when low-category monsters appear in higher-category dungeons.
maximumCount: hard cap.

Swarm count

swarmSpawnTuning controls monsters with behavior "swarm":

minimumCount: minimum pack size.
extraPerPartyMember: extra count per additional party member.
extraPerCategoryGap: more low-category swarm monsters in higher-category dungeons.
absoluteMaximum: hard cap.

Bosses

heroNeedsDungeonBoss(hero) currently returns true on even levels, such as 2, 4, 6, 8.
Bosses spawn in the exit room and use bossMonsterTagGroups from the theme.

Monster scaling

applyMonsterCategoryScaling handles scaling lower-category monsters upward. Tune HP and attack scaling there if low-category monsters feel too weak or too strong in higher dungeons.
Scaling can also grant multiattack to promoted monsters: large category gaps can add 2 or 3 attacks, and boss-tagged monsters can reach 4 attacks in category 10 dungeons. Swarm monsters are excluded because their scaling already comes from spawning more bodies. Promoted multiattack uses a lower per-hit damage multiplier than native single-hit scaling so the monster becomes more active without turning every hit into a full original strike.

5e CR comparison note

Reference point: category N is roughly treated as D&D 5e CR 2N for broad comparison only.

Current normal monster averages compared to the official-style Monster Statistics by Challenge Rating table:

cat 1 ~= CR 2: 13% target HP, -0.1 AC, +0.9 attack, 30% target damage
cat 2 ~= CR 4: 21% target HP, +0.3 AC, +0.2 attack, 25% target damage
cat 3 ~= CR 6: 29% target HP, +0.2 AC, +0.4 attack, 24% target damage
cat 4 ~= CR 8: 39% target HP, +0.7 AC, +0.4 attack, 26% target damage
cat 5 ~= CR 10: 47% target HP, +0.4 AC, +1.5 attack, 28% target damage
cat 6 ~= CR 12: 55% target HP, +1.1 AC, +1.6 attack, 30% target damage
cat 7 ~= CR 14: 64% target HP, +1.5 AC, +2.7 attack, 30% target damage
cat 8 ~= CR 16: 74% target HP, +2.4 AC, +2.7 attack, 33% target damage
cat 9 ~= CR 18: 82% target HP, +2.6 AC, +2.5 attack, 33% target damage
cat 10 ~= CR 20: 90% target HP, +3.8 AC, +4.0 attack, 32% target damage

Interpretation:
Normal monsters are intentionally much softer and less lethal than official CR-equivalent 5e monsters. They have low HP at early and mid categories and only approach official HP at cat 9-10. Their damage is consistently low, around one quarter to one third of official CR expectations. They do, however, have slightly high AC and high attack bonuses, especially in late categories, so they can feel accurate and hard to hit without dealing official-level burst damage.

Bosses are closer on HP and exceed official HP expectations at high categories, but boss damage is still below official CR expectations. This supports the current design of many weaker enemies plus occasional durable bosses, rather than one monster equaling a full tabletop CR-equivalent encounter.

Current conclusion:
This is acceptable for now. Do not automatically raise all monster damage to official CR values unless the whole encounter economy, party size, rest frequency, boss design, and loot pacing are rebalanced together.

Monster XP progression target

Enemy monster XP is tuned for dungeon-flow progression, not official CR-equivalent XP.

Current target:

- A full average dungeon should roughly grant one level.
- The full 1-20 climb should take about 19 average dungeons at the current spawn tuning.

Assumptions for the progression estimate:

- 4 active heroes.
- XP split evenly across the party.
- Average dungeon has about 6.5 monster encounters.
- Normal encounter count for 4 heroes is about 2.95 monsters.
- Even-level dungeons include 1 boss encounter plus the remaining normal encounters.
- Recruits, hirelings, summons, XP 0 entries, and the monster template are excluded from enemy XP averages.

XP is now tuned to the runtime encounter economy rather than official CR-equivalent XP. This makes both normal and boss dungeons land near one level per full average dungeon.

Loot from normal monsters

createLootForMonster controls normal and boss drop chances.

Current normal monster chances:
healing potion: 5%
mundane equipment: 2%
treasure: 2%
magic item: about 1-2%, based on category

These are intentionally low. Raise them slowly if fights feel unrewarding.

Boss loot

Current boss chances:
healing potion: 30%
mundane equipment: 18%
treasure: 75%
magic item: 20% + 8% per category, capped at 55%

Bosses are the main source of magic items.

Chest loot

randomChestLoot controls chest rewards.

Current chest logic:
normal equipment/potion/ammo rolls first
treasure chance increases by category
magic chance is rare and increases by category

Chest spawn chance is theme-controlled:

furnitureSpawnChances: {
  chest: 0.2,
}

Treasure and magic value caps

maxLootPriceGpForCategory controls how valuable loot can be:

category 1: 2,500 gp
category 2: 9,000 gp
category 3: 35,000 gp
category 4: 90,000 gp
category 5+: 200,000 gp

If high-level loot appears too early, lower these caps.
If loot feels boring at high levels, raise these caps or add more items under the current cap.

Drop weights

Magic items use item.loot.dropWeight.
Treasure uses item.treasure.dropWeight.

Higher weight means more common. Lower weight means rarer.

Price also affects default weight in the item helper files, so expensive items are naturally rarer.

Merchant rules

Magic and treasure are loot-only for buying:
store.buyable: false

Treasure can be sold at full value using:
sell.rate: 1

Magic can be sold but is not currently buyable from the normal merchant.
