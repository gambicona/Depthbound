Dwarven Smithy Questchain Concept
Analysis-only design note, 2026-05-21

Working title
The Dwarven Smithy: The Ember Oath

Core pitch
A lost dwarven forge-path under the home mines is waking up again. The player starts with practical mine work: coal, ore, missing apprentices, broken hoists, and a smithy that cannot be relit. Each dungeon restores one missing part of an old dwarven forge rite. The chain starts underground in the Embervein Deepworks, then pushes through planar breach-forges into the Crucible of Flame and Crucible of Stone, then returns to the Deepworks for a final reforging.

The result should unlock a whole new main dungeon path: the Dwarven Smithy / Ember Oath path. It can also unlock smithy home upgrades, special forge services, companion NPCs, and later higher-tier Embervein routes.


Theme findings

1. The Embervein Deepworks

Best use:
- The main spine of the questchain.
- Feels like a dwarf-built forge-mine that has been overrun by elementals, devils, broken machinery, smoke, steam, and industrial hazards.
- Excellent for the first 3 dungeons and the final return dungeon.

Key furniture and terrain:
- Minecart, ore-cart, coal-pile, mine-support-beam, powder-keg, conveyor-belt, drill-rig.
- Anvil, forge-furnace, cooling-trough, molten-slag, tool-rack.
- Chain-hoist, steam-pipe, pressure-valve, broken-gear-assembly, oil-barrel, locked-supply-locker.
- Resource nodes: stone-outcrop, ore-vein, coal-seam, plus thematic gem/brimstone/cave nodes.
- Terrain supports lava, water, acid, and healing pools. The lava/water mix is ideal for forge rooms, cooling rooms, and puzzle gates.

Monster feel:
- Early: soot rats, coal-dust goblins, gear scarabs, mephits, ore crawlers, chain guards.
- Mid: slag slimes, animated anvils, steam gremlins, magma lopes, saboteurs, gear horrors.
- Late: huge machines, golems, drakes, titan-scale ore and forge creatures.

Important large monsters already useful for spectacle:
- redValveEngine, size 2.
- adamantineMineGolem, size 2.
- conveyorBeltDevourer, size 2.
- volcanicDeepDrake, size 2.
- forgeheartColossus, size 3.
- oreveinTitan, size 3.
- pressureCoreElemental, size 2.
- primordialSlagAncient, catastropheEngine, worldAnvilDragon, theEmberveinAwakened, size 3.


2. The Crucible of Flame

Best use:
- A planar detour after the party has learned the forge problem is not just mechanical.
- Strong fit for a quest about Cinderheart Coal, living flame, or bargaining with fire spirits.

Key furniture and terrain:
- Heavy lava usage.
- Forge-furnace, molten-slag, coal-pile, steam-vent, gas-vent, basalt-column, brimstone-cluster, infernal-brazier, ash-drift.
- Resource nodes: brimstone, ore, gem, stone.

Monster feel:
- Ember wisps, cinder sprites, smoke mephits, lava-glass skitterers, coal-heart igniters.
- Later: fire myrmidons, ash stranglers, magma hounds, salamanders, obsidian smelters, cinderstorm dancers.

Use in chain:
- Dungeon 4 should be the cleanest fit.
- The boss should be more ritual/forge-based than generic fire-monster-based, for example a Furnace-Core Myrmidon guarding the coal that can relight the dwarven forge.


3. The Crucible of Stone

Best use:
- The second planar detour.
- A quest about the anvil-stone, true names, foundation runes, and weight/oath/patience.

Key furniture and terrain:
- Unstable-stone terrain.
- Stalagmites, stalactite clusters, crystal-growth, razor-crystal-patch, mineral-vein, cave-in-rubble, narrow-rock-column, loose-scree, fallen-sandstone-block.
- Resource nodes: stone, ore, gem, cave.

Monster feel:
- Pebblelings, dust mephitlings, mud crawlers, shale hounds, claybinders, graven idols.
- Mid tier: crystalback maulers, grave-soil elementals, iron-ore myrmidons, obsidian razors, granite bulwarks.

Use in chain:
- Dungeon 5 should be the best fit.
- This should feel slower and heavier than the flame chapter: pressure plates, weight puzzles, unstable floor paths, and a boss room where the party must control movement.


XP pacing and dungeon categories

Assumption:
- Four XP-eligible heroes.
- Starting at level 2 with about 300 XP each.
- The game category mapping is roughly category = ceil(hero level / 2), so level 2 is category 1, levels 3-4 are category 2, levels 5-6 are category 3, levels 7-8 are category 4.
- If a player-controlled companion also receives XP, these totals should be increased or the companion should join after rewards are paid. To keep pacing clean, rescued allies should usually be non-XP escorts during their rescue dungeon, then become home NPCs or optional companions afterward.

XP target table:

Dungeon 1:
- Party starts level 2, 300 XP each.
- Target category: 1.
- XP needed to reach level 3: 600 each, 2400 party total.
- Suggested split: 1600 monster XP, 800 quest XP.
- Party ends at level 3, 900 XP each.

Dungeon 2:
- Party starts level 3, 900 XP each.
- Target category: 2.
- XP needed to reach level 4: 1800 each, 7200 party total.
- Suggested split: 5000 monster XP, 2200 quest XP.
- Party ends at level 4, 2700 XP each.

Dungeon 3:
- Party starts level 4, 2700 XP each.
- Target category: 2, with a carefully staged category 3 forge-guardian boss.
- XP needed to reach level 5: 3800 each, 15200 party total.
- Suggested split: 10500 monster XP, 4700 quest XP.
- Party ends at level 5, 6500 XP each.

Dungeon 4:
- Party starts level 5, 6500 XP each.
- Target category: 3.
- XP needed to reach level 6: 7500 each, 30000 party total.
- Suggested split: 22000 monster XP, 8000 quest XP.
- Party ends at level 6, 14000 XP each.

Dungeon 5:
- Party starts level 6, 14000 XP each.
- Target category: 3.
- XP needed to reach level 7: 9000 each, 36000 party total.

- Party ends at level 7, 23000 XP each.

Dungeon 6:

- Party ends at level 8, 34000 XP each.

Design note:
These are pacing targets, not exact random-generation guarantees. If these become real authored dungeons, each dungeon should have fixed encounter lists and a completion reward so the final total lands near the table.


Questchain structure

Quest 1: The Coal Debt
Theme: Embervein Deepworks
Party: four level 2 heroes
Dungeon category: 1
Primary goal: recover the stolen bellows-crank and find out why the home smithy will not light.
Secondary goal: rescue a dwarven apprentice from a locked supply room.
Unlock: the smithy NPC returns home and opens the first Embervein route.

Story:
The local dwarven smith has an old forge that should burn from mine-coal, but every ember dies blue-black. A mine ledger reveals that the "first coal" was claimed by Sootbeard, a corrupt shift boss who has turned the lower mine into a toll gate. The party descends to recover the bellows-crank and the unpaid coal mark.

Potential companion:
- Borin Emberhand, young dwarf apprentice.
- Recommended role: noncombat rescue NPC during the dungeon, home smithing helper afterward.
- Later unlock: can identify ore and reduce upgrade costs, or provide simple smithy jobs.

Special lock ideas:
- Locked-supply-locker with label "Shift Locker".
- Prompt: "What feeds the first heat?"
- Answer: COAL
- Optional boss door prompt: "First heat, then..."
- Answer: HAMMER

Room outline:
1. Sooted Mine Mouth
- Mine-support-beams, coal-piles, one coal-seam.
- Monsters: 2 Soot-Tunnel Rats, 1 Bent-Gear Scarab.
- Purpose: teaches coal/mining furniture and small enemies.

2. Broken Cart Switch
- Minecart, ore-cart, loose-scree, pressure-valve.
- Monsters: 2 Coal-Dust Goblins.
- Optional puzzle: push minecart to block a side tunnel or break a weak support.

3. Coal Seam Hollow
- Coal-seam, bigRock, powder-keg.
- Monsters: 2 Soot-Tunnel Rats, 1 Coal-Dust Goblin.
- Optional goal: gather clean coal for bonus smithy reward.

4. Chain-Hoist Cage
- Chain-hoist, locked-supply-locker, tool-rack.
- Monsters: 1 Bent-Gear Scarab, 1 Coal-Dust Goblin.
- Rescue Borin here.

5. The Coal Weigh-House, boss
- Furniture: chain-hoist, minecart track, powder-keg, ore-cart, coal-piles.
- Boss composition:
  - Sootbeard Shift Boss, 130 XP.
  - 2 Coal-Dust Goblins, 110 XP.
  - 2 Bent-Gear Scarabs, 130 XP.
  - 1 delayed Soot-Tunnel Rat wave, 45 XP.
  - Boss room total: 415 XP.
- Boss behavior:
  - Sootbeard starts behind half cover near the ledger scale.
  - Goblins try to shove or lure heroes near carts and powder-keg.
  - Scarabs threaten the backline and make the room feel busy.

Expected XP:
- Monster target: about 1600 XP across all rooms.
- Quest reward: 800 XP.
- Party gain: 2400 XP.


Quest 2: The Chain-Hoist Rebellion
Theme: Embervein Deepworks
Party: four level 3 heroes
Dungeon category: 2
Primary goal: seize the Deepworks route-map and the first portal plate.
Secondary goal: free a captured dwarven chainwright.
Unlock: the second Smithy path dungeon and the first visible "old forge gate" on the map.

Story:
Borin identifies the recovered bellows-crank as only one part of an older gate-forge. The route-map was taken by the Chain-Hoist Overseer, who uses dwarven lift machinery to move prisoners, ore, and furnace fuel through the mine without opening the main roads.

Potential companion:
- Kelda Chainwright, dwarf warrior/expert.
- Recommended role: rescue NPC during dungeon, then optional player-controlled companion after the dungeon.
- If XP sharing is implemented for her, add 25 percent more encounter XP or delay her XP eligibility until after the dungeon.

Special lock ideas:
- Hoist control door label: "Counterweight Lock".
- Prompt: "The empty hook rises when the full hook..."
- Answer: FALLS
- Object lock on map case:
  - Prompt: "A chain is strongest at the..."
  - Answer: LINK

Room outline:
1. Abandoned Lift Landing
- Chain-hoist, mine-support-beams, ore-cart.
- Monsters: 2 Furnace Mephits, 1 Ore-Shell Crawler.

2. Gear Ratchet Hall
- Broken-gear-assembly, conveyor-belt, steam-pipe.
- Monsters: 2 Deep Dwarf Chain Guards.
- Hazard: broken gears punish careless pathing.

3. Prison Hoist
- Chain-hoist cage, locked-supply-locker, pressure-valve.
- Monsters: 1 Ore-Shell Crawler, 2 Furnace Mephits.

4. Furnace Toll Office
- Forge-furnace, tool-rack, reinforced-crate.
- Monsters: 2 Deep Dwarf Chain Guards, 1 Furnace Mephit.

5. Map Case Crossing
- Narrow bridge over lava/water channel, steam-pipes, pressure-valves.

6. The Suspended Weigh-Forge, boss
- Furniture: chain-hoists, conveyor-belt, powder-kegs, pressure-valves.
- Boss composition:
  - Chain-Hoist Overseer, 430 XP.

  - Boss room total: 1370 XP.
- Boss behavior:
  - Overseer holds center under chain-hoists.

  - Mephits punish clustering and try to ignite powder-kegs.

Expected XP:
- Monster target: about 5000 XP.

Quest 3: The Furnace That Remembers
Theme: Embervein Deepworks, with the first Crucible of Flame bleed-through
Party: four level 4 heroes
Dungeon category: 2, with a controlled category 3 boss encounter
Primary goal: rekindle the planes-lantern using the old ward furnace.
Secondary goal: learn the words that open the elemental routes.
Unlock: Crucible of Flame and Crucible of Stone branches.

Story:
The route-map points to a ward furnace that remembers every oath sworn in the first smithy. It has not gone cold. It has gone angry. The party needs its living flame, but the furnace believes the dwarves abandoned their oath and sends animated tools and slag-things to test them.

Potential companion:
- No permanent companion needed here.
- Borin or Kelda can appear in quest text as the person who prepares the planes-lantern at home.

Special lock ideas:
- Furnace door label: "Oath Furnace".
- Prompt: "What comes between tongs and blade?"
- Answer: HEAT
- Final ward prompt:
  - "Speak the order of craft."
  - Answer: HEAT HAMMER QUENCH

Room outline:
1. Quench Gallery
- Cooling-trough, forge-furnace, steam-pipe.
- Monsters: 2 Furnace Mephits, 1 Deep Dwarf Chain Guard.
- Puzzle: cooling trough can temporarily suppress steam/fire hazards.

2. Slag Runoff
- Molten-slag, coal-pile, gas-vent.
- Monsters: 2 Slag Slimes.

3. The Anvil Choir
- Multiple anvils and tool-racks.
- Monsters: 1 Animated Anvil, 2 Steam-Pipe Gremlins.
- Optional goal: repair one anvil instead of destroying all constructs for bonus quest text.

4. Valve Psalm
- Pressure-valves, steam-pipes, conveyor-belt.
- Monsters: 2 Steam-Pipe Gremlins, 1 Slag Slime.
- Puzzle: pressure valves open the boss door if set in the right order.

5. The Remembering Furnace, boss
- Furniture: forge-furnace, cooling-troughs, molten-slag, pressure-valves.
- Boss composition:
  - Furnace-Ward Guardian, 1050 XP.
  - 1 Animated Anvil, 470 XP.
  - 2 Steam-Pipe Gremlins, 840 XP.
  - 2 Slag Slimes, 760 XP.
  - Boss room total: 3120 XP.
- Boss behavior:
  - Guardian controls center and punishes anyone standing near the furnace mouth.
  - Gremlins operate valves or create steam cover.
  - Slimes make escape paths sticky and dangerous.

Expected XP:
- Monster target: about 10500 XP.
- Quest reward: 4700 XP.
- Party gain: 15200 XP.


Quest 4: Cinderside Bargain
Theme: Crucible of Flame
Party: four level 5 heroes
Dungeon category: 3
Primary goal: recover Cinderheart Coal from the elemental flame side of the old forge.
Secondary goal: choose whether to bargain with, free, or defeat a dissident flame-worker.
Unlock: first true forge upgrade, and access to the Stone route.

Story:
The planes-lantern opens into the Crucible of Flame. The dwarven forge cannot be relit by normal coal because its first fire was made with Cinderheart Coal: a living ember mined from the border between craft and wildfire. The current keeper will not give it up unless the party proves they can control heat instead of merely surviving it.

Potential companion:
- Ilyra Ashglass, fire genasi or salamander dissident.
- Recommended role: temporary AI ally in the dungeon.
- After the dungeon she can become a home contact, fire-resistance vendor, or optional spellcaster companion.
- If kept as a full party member immediately, increase later XP budgets.

Special lock ideas:
- Brazier lock label: "Three Flames".
- Prompt: "Name the fire that does not consume."
- Answer: FORGE
- Optional moral lock:
  - Free Ilyra by extinguishing three infernal braziers instead of killing her jailers.

Room outline:
1. Ash Door Landing
- Ash-drift, brimstone-cluster, infernal-brazier.
- Monsters: 2 Smoke Mephit Sneaks, 2 Lava-Glass Skitterers.

2. Brimstone Counting Floor
- Brimstone-crust/resource nodes, basalt-columns, gas-vents.
- Monsters: 2 Coal-Heart Igniters, 1 Ash Paraelemental Strangler.

3. The Three Braziers
- Infernal-braziers, molten-slag, cooling-trough equivalent if present.
- Monsters: 2 Lesser Fire Myrmidons.

4. Ilyra's Glass Cage
- Lava channel, brimstone clusters, special locked object.

Expected XP:

Quest 5: The Stone That Names the Forge

Dungeon category: 3
Primary goal: recover the True-Name Anvilstone.

The Cinderheart Coal burns too hot for any normal anvil. The smith needs the True-Name Anvilstone, a foundation stone from the elemental plane of earth. It is not guarded by anger, but by memory and weight. The dungeon asks whether the party can carry an oath without breaking it.

Quest 6: The First Heat, The Last Chain
Theme: Embervein Deepworks final return, with Flame and Stone elements bleeding in
Party: four level 7 heroes
Dungeon category: 4
Primary goal: carry Cinderheart Coal and the True-Name Anvilstone back to the first foundry and reforge the Ember Oath.
Secondary goal: decide what kind of smithy the settlement becomes.
Unlock: the full Dwarven Smithy main dungeon path, plus long-term smithing/crafting rewards.

Story:
With fire and stone restored, the party returns to the Deepworks through the original foundry road. The mine has changed. The machinery remembers the old betrayal, and something called Slagmaw has gathered every broken gear, sabotaged keg, and molten vein into a final refusal. The party is not just clearing a dungeon. They are choosing whether the forge becomes a weapon, a shelter, or a bridge.

Potential final choice:
- Oath of the Shield: smithy upgrades armor, resistance, home defenses.
- Oath of the Blade: smithy upgrades weapons and damage.
- Oath of the Gate: smithy improves dungeon path access, planar materials, and travel.

Special lock ideas:
- Final foundry door label: "The Ember Oath".
- Prompt: "Speak the craft in order."
- Answer: HEAT HAMMER QUENCH
- Optional forge choice is presented after boss defeat, not as a lock.

Room outline:
1. Returned Lift Road
- Chain-hoists, conveyor-belts, mine-support-beams.
- Monsters: 2 Magma-Vein Lopers, 1 Powder-Keg Saboteur.

2. Saboteur Magazine

3. Broken Gear Chapel

4. The Cooling Argument
- Cooling-troughs, lava/water channels, pressure-valves.

5. The First Foundry, boss
- Furniture: forge-furnaces, molten-slag, conveyor-belts, chain-hoists, oil-barrels, powder-kegs, pressure-valves.

  - Slagmaw Brute, 2400 XP.
  - 1 Broken-Gear Horror, 1100 XP.

  - 2 Powder-Keg Saboteurs, 1800 XP.

Optional five-dungeon variant

If the chain needs to be shorter, combine Quest 4 and Quest 5 into one larger "Twin Crucibles" dungeon. The party enters the Crucible of Flame first, steals or bargains for Cinderheart Coal, then descends through cooling basalt into the Crucible of Stone for the Anvilstone. This is elegant thematically, but worse for XP pacing because it jumps from level 5 to level 7 territory inside one long dungeon. The six-dungeon version is cleaner.

Implementation considerations for later

1. Authoring versus random generation
- The questline wants authored boss rooms and special locks. It should not rely only on random dungeon generation if XP pacing and story beats matter.
- Random generation can still fill side rooms, but key rooms should be fixed templates or explicitly listed encounter rooms.

2. Companion XP
- Cleanest approach: rescued allies do not join XP splitting until after their rescue dungeon.
- If they become full heroes immediately, XP budgets should be recalculated for five XP recipients.

3. Special locks
- This questchain is a strong showcase for the new special locks.
- Use repeated forge language so the player learns the ritual:
  - COAL
  - HAMMER
  - HEAT
  - HEAT HAMMER QUENCH
  - WEIGHT
  - STONE

4. Large-monster system fit
- Early dungeons should avoid many size 2+ monsters so the new path does not become cramped at level 2-4.
- Dungeon 5 can introduce one size 2 boss-adjacent creature with the Graven Idol Guardian.
- Dungeon 6 can use a bigger, room-shaping boss fight but should still be mostly size 1 enemies plus one large wall-like creature.
- Later post-chain Embervein dungeons can fully show off size 2 and size 3 monsters.

5. Best recurring furniture mechanics
- Pressure-valves should control steam or lava safety.
- Cooling-troughs should counter forge heat.
- Chain-hoists should serve as puzzle locks and boss hazards.
- Minecarts and ore-carts should enable push/block/shove play.
- Powder-kegs and oil-barrels should make fire enemies tactically dangerous.

6. Main risk
- XP and encounter density will need tuning. The boss compositions above are strong set-piece anchors, but the total dungeon XP targets require additional side encounters or explicit quest XP.
- Companion behavior and XP splitting must be decided before implementation, otherwise the leveling math will drift.


Clean storyline

This is the questchain as it should feel to the player, without the implementation notes.

The party begins in the home settlement with a simple problem: the dwarven smithy cannot work anymore. The forge still stands, the tools are still there, and the smith knows the old trade, but every fire lit in the hearth turns weak and blue-black. Ore comes out brittle. Repairs fail. Weapons lose their edge after a single expedition.

The local dwarven smith believes the problem starts in the mine below the settlement. The old dwarves did not build this smithy as an ordinary workshop. It was once connected to a deeper forge-road called the Ember Oath, a dwarven route that joined mine, flame, and stone into one sacred craft. That route was sealed generations ago after a disaster. Now something below has woken up and is starving the smithy from underneath.


Chapter 1: The Coal Debt

The party is sent into the lower mine to recover the first thing a forge needs: clean coal and working bellows. At first it looks like a local mine problem. Soot rats infest the tunnels, coal-dust goblins are stealing supplies, and the lower shifts have stopped sending carts upward.

The party discovers that a corrupt shift boss, Sootbeard, has claimed the old coal stores and stolen the bellows-crank from the settlement smithy. He is not just hoarding fuel. He is collecting anything marked with the old dwarven forge-rune.

In the mine the party rescues Borin Emberhand, a young dwarven apprentice locked in a supply cage after he followed the stolen equipment underground. Borin recognizes that the stolen bellows-crank is part of an older machine, not just a smithy tool.

The party defeats Sootbeard in the coal weigh-house, recovers the bellows-crank, and brings Borin home. The smithy can now breathe again, but its fire still will not hold. Borin explains why: the bellows are only one piece of a sealed forge-road. To restore the smithy, they need to find the route-map of the old Embervein Deepworks.

What changes:
- Borin returns home.
- The dwarven smithy becomes an active quest hub.
- The party learns that the problem is larger than stolen supplies.
- The next goal is clear: find the Deepworks route-map.


Chapter 2: The Chain-Hoist Rebellion

The recovered bellows-crank bears a mark from the Deepworks lift system. The party follows it into a deeper part of the mine where old chain-hoists, ore lifts, and furnace carts still move on their own. The machinery is being controlled by the Chain-Hoist Overseer, who has turned the lift network into a prison and toll road.

The Overseer controls the route-map, because whoever controls the lifts controls access to the sealed foundries. He also holds Kelda Chainwright, a dwarven engineer who tried to reopen the route herself. Kelda knows how to read the old lift marks, but she cannot escape without help.

The party fights through lift landings, gear halls, steam pipes, and prison hoists. They solve the counterweight lock, free Kelda, and confront the Overseer in the suspended weigh-forge.

After the battle, Kelda reads the route-map. It does not point straight to a treasure vault or boss chamber. It points to an oath furnace: a dwarven ward-forge that remembers who is allowed to pass. If the party wants the smithy restored, they need that furnace to accept the old oath again.

What changes:
- Kelda returns home or becomes an optional companion.
- The party gains the Deepworks route-map.
- The sealed forge-road becomes a real path on the dungeon map.
- The next goal is clear: relight the oath furnace.


Chapter 3: The Furnace That Remembers

The party enters the old ward-furnace. This dungeon should feel different from the first two. It is still in the Deepworks, but it is no longer only a mine. It is a place of memory: anvils move like guards, slag rises like anger, and steam pipes hiss old warnings through the walls.

The furnace has not forgotten the dwarves. It remembers them too well. It remembers the day the Ember Oath was broken and refuses to feed the settlement smithy until someone proves they understand the craft.

The party must learn and use the forge order: heat, hammer, quench. The special locks are not random riddles here. They are ritual checks. The party is not hacking through a door; they are speaking the language of the forge.

At the heart of the dungeon, the Furnace-Ward Guardian tests them. When defeated or pacified, the guardian gives the party a living spark and reveals the truth: the smithy cannot be restored with mine coal alone. The original forge was built from three promises:

- Coal from the mine, for work.
- Fire from the Crucible of Flame, for transformation.
- Stone from the Crucible of Stone, for memory.

The ward furnace opens two planar routes. The party now has the living spark, but it needs a true fuel and a true anvil before the settlement forge can be relit.

What changes:
- The party gains the planes-lantern or living spark.
- The Crucible of Flame and Crucible of Stone become reachable.
- The recurring forge ritual is established.
- The next goal is clear: claim Cinderheart Coal from the fire plane.


Chapter 4: Cinderside Bargain

The party steps out of the mine and into the Crucible of Flame. This should feel like the first big expansion of the questchain. The party is no longer just repairing a local dwarven workplace. They are entering the elemental source of dwarven craft.

The Cinderheart Coal is guarded by a fire court that does not respect ownership, only mastery. The flame creatures believe the old dwarves stole their fire and then abandoned the agreement that kept it balanced. To them, the settlement smithy going cold is justice.

The party can approach this chapter as a theft, a trial, or a bargain. Ilyra Ashglass, a dissident flame-worker, offers another way. She says the current keeper is not protecting the Cinderheart Coal. He is hoarding it and feeding on the broken oath. If the party frees her and extinguishes the three infernal braziers, she will help them take the coal without letting the Crucible collapse into rage.

The party fights through ash doors, brimstone counting floors, and brazier chambers. In the Cinderheart Vault they defeat the Furnace-Core Myrmidon and claim the living coal.

The Cinderheart Coal burns bright enough to relight the smithy, but it is too powerful for an ordinary anvil. If placed in the settlement forge now, it would crack the hearth and burn the smithy down. The party needs the stone that can hold the fire's name.

What changes:
- The party gains Cinderheart Coal.
- Ilyra can become a contact, temporary ally, or later companion.
- Fire-aspected smithy rewards can unlock.
- The next goal is clear: recover the True-Name Anvilstone.


Chapter 5: The Stone That Names the Forge

The party enters the Crucible of Stone. This chapter should feel calmer than the fire plane, but not safer. The threat is weight, patience, and consequence. The dungeon asks the party to move carefully, solve weight locks, cross unstable stone, and earn passage rather than simply overpowering everything.

The True-Name Anvilstone is not treasure in the normal sense. It is a foundation stone that remembers every oath struck upon it. The old dwarves used it to keep the forge fire from becoming wildfire.

In the stone halls the party finds Garn Stone-Song, a dwarven mason who came searching for the Anvilstone years ago and became half-bound into the earth plane. Garn explains the real history: the old Ember Oath was broken when the first smiths tried to use the forge-road for conquest instead of craft. The furnace sealed itself, the planes withdrew their gifts, and the route was buried.

The party rescues Garn and reaches the Name-Hewn Vault. There they defeat the Quake-Fist Champion and the Graven Idol Guardian, proving they can carry the weight of the oath without breaking it.

When the party claims the True-Name Anvilstone, the living spark from the ward furnace and the Cinderheart Coal react together. The final route opens by itself: the way back to the First Foundry under the Embervein Deepworks.

What changes:
- Garn returns home as a mason or forge-worker.
- The party gains the True-Name Anvilstone.
- Stone-aspected smithy rewards can unlock.
- The next goal is clear: return to the First Foundry and restore the Ember Oath.


Chapter 6: The First Heat, The Last Chain

The party returns to the Embervein Deepworks, but now the mine knows what they are carrying. The old forge-road wakes up. Lifts move, pressure valves scream, lava channels reopen, and every broken machine between the settlement and the First Foundry tries to stop them.

This final dungeon is not about finding another missing object. It is about bringing the three pieces together:

- The coal debt has been paid.
- The fire has been earned.
- The stone has been named.

Slagmaw Brute waits in the First Foundry, not as a random monster, but as the shape of the broken oath. It is made of slag, sabotage, old machinery, and everything the Deepworks became after the dwarves abandoned their responsibility. It does not want the forge destroyed. It wants the forge to stay angry.

The party fights through saboteur magazines, broken gear chapels, cooling channels, and the final foundry floor. In the boss fight, Slagmaw tries to knock heroes into molten lanes while saboteurs threaten powder-kegs and pressure valves. The room should feel like the whole questchain coming together: coal, flame, stone, chains, valves, water, slag, and hammer-work.

When Slagmaw falls, the party sets the True-Name Anvilstone into the old hearth, places the Cinderheart Coal beneath it, and uses the restored bellows-crank to draw the first true heat. The forge lights properly for the first time in generations.

Then the party chooses what the restored smithy becomes:

- Shield: the smithy becomes a place of protection, armor, resistance, and home defense.
- Blade: the smithy becomes a place of weapons, damage, and aggressive upgrades.
- Gate: the smithy becomes a place of routes, planar materials, and deeper dungeon access.

This choice should not erase the other rewards, but it should define the strongest branch.

What changes:
- The Dwarven Smithy main dungeon path fully unlocks.
- The settlement gains a restored forge identity.
- Borin, Kelda, Ilyra, and Garn can all have roles at home if rescued.
- Later high-level Embervein content becomes justified: the party did not just find a dungeon, they reopened an ancient industrial-planar road.


Why the chain makes sense

The quest starts small because the party is low level and because the player needs a grounded reason to care: the smithy is broken, supplies are missing, and someone is trapped below.

Each dungeon answers the question created by the previous one:

1. Why is the smithy failing?
Because the forge-road underneath it is sealed and its coal/bellows system has been stolen.

2. Where does the forge-road go?
Through the old Deepworks lift map, now controlled by the Chain-Hoist Overseer.

3. Why is the route sealed?
Because the oath furnace remembers a broken dwarven promise and demands the old craft order.

4. Why is normal coal not enough?
Because the original forge needs living fire from the Crucible of Flame.

5. Why can the party not just use that fire immediately?
Because living fire needs the True-Name Anvilstone to contain it.

6. What is the final enemy?
The broken oath itself, given monster shape in the First Foundry.

The player is always doing a concrete thing:
- Recover the bellows-crank.
- Get the route-map.
- Relight the ward furnace.
- Claim the Cinderheart Coal.
- Recover the True-Name Anvilstone.
- Reforge the Ember Oath.

That keeps the questchain understandable while still letting it grow from mine trouble into elemental-plane myth.
