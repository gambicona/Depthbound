# Barrow Crown Religion Handout Placement Plan

Purpose: plan which collectible religion handouts belong in the existing Barrow Crown dungeons.

Important direction:

The Barrow Crown should **not** feel like a general tour of every deity.

This campaign is about crown-law, command, oath-dead, royal burial, erased names, military guilt, and the possibility that Kharovan is not a true god at all. Most religion handouts here should therefore be:

- **Kharovan**: public crown doctrine, royal oaths, command, law, duty, propaganda.
- **Naevra**: burial records, names, erased identities, Gravebinder warnings.
- **The Hollow Below**: only later, as corruption or something answering from beneath the crown.

Other deities should be rare or absent. Lioran, Maera, Hadrin, Orund, Thessa, and the Crucible Powers can appear in other campaigns where they belong more naturally.


## What This Place Was

The Barrow Crown sites were probably not ordinary temples.

They were a royal-burial and military-command complex from the old Crownroad Concord. Their purpose was to bind kings, officers, soldiers, oaths, banners, death records, succession rights, and emergency command rites into one system.

That means the faith here was not warm village religion. It was official, solemn, administrative, and dangerous.

The main religious layers should be:

1. **Official Kharovan worship**
   - lawful command
   - military duty
   - royal succession
   - oath-binding
   - command over soldiers and war-dead

2. **Naevran burial administration**
   - names of the dead
   - grave ledgers
   - erased records
   - warnings about nameless corpses
   - rituals for preventing undead misuse

3. **Hollow contamination**
   - not present openly at first
   - appears later where the crown has become hollow
   - suggests that something below has learned to answer through command, law, and buried authority

## Revised Main Placement Plan

This version keeps the campaign focused.

Recommended count:

- 1-2 handouts per dungeon.
- Mostly Kharovan.
- Naevra appears where records/names/burial matter.
- Hollow appears only as late corruption.


### Dungeon 1: The Robbed Tomb

This dungeon should introduce burial failure and crown authority without revealing the deeper corruption yet.

#### Chamber of Extinguished Candles

Handout:

`religion-naevra-grave-candle-instruction`

Why it fits:

This is the best first handout.

The room already suggests failed rites: extinguished candles, tomb-darkness, and burial gone wrong. Naevra belongs here because the first lesson should be that the dead are dangerous when rites and names fail.

This also prepares the player to read undead as a religious/legal problem, not only a monster problem.

#### Reliquary of Lesser Crowns

Handout:

`religion-kharovan-royal-oath-prayer`

Why it fits:

This introduces Kharovan in his respectable public form.

The Reliquary of Lesser Crowns should feel like a place where minor rulers, oath-officers, or royal retainers were ritually tied to the greater crown system. The prayer should sound noble at this point. Let players believe Kharovan might simply be stern and lawful.


### Dungeon 2: The Hall of Oathbreakers

This dungeon should be strongly Kharovan and Naevra. It is about soldiers, broken vows, erased names, and command failure.

#### The Great Hall of the Oathless Dead

Handout:

`religion-kharovan-buried-crown-litany`

Why it fits:

The litany belongs in a hall of oathless dead because it sounds like the official ritual response to military failure.

It should feel like something soldiers were made to recite before burial or punishment: duty under soil, law under bone, command under silence.

#### Scriptorium of Erased Names

Handout:

`religion-naevra-ledger-heresy`

Why it fits:

This is exactly where Naevra should appear.

The room is not just a library; it is an archive wound. Names have been erased. Records failed. The handout's claim that Naevra keeps the Ledger but did not write the first page fits the room and hints at the Anchor Tools without making the dungeon about all gods.

Optional alternative:

`religion-naevra-record-of-a-stolen-name`

Use this instead if you want the room to feel more like a case file than a theological heresy.


### Dungeon 3: The Sunken Ossuary

This dungeon is Naevra-heavy. It is about nameless dead, drowned records, and identity washed away.

#### Sluice of the Nameless Dead

Handout:

`religion-naevra-nameless-warning`

Why it fits:

This is the most obvious match in the whole campaign.

The handout warns against burying the nameless under borrowed names. In a drowned ossuary, names have literally been washed loose from bodies. This should feel like a Gravebinder warning that was ignored.

#### The Drowned Legion's Rest

Handout:

`religion-naevra-soldier-name-tag`

Why it fits:

This connects Naevra to the military dead and starts bridging Dungeon 3 back toward Kharovan.

The soldier name-tag has the right tone: a dead officer or soldier trying to preserve identity against future command. It makes the party wonder whether these soldiers are still people, still records, or just assets claimed by the Crown.


### Dungeon 4: The Grave-Market

This dungeon should feel like religious administration has become commerce and crime.

Use mostly Naevra here, with one Kharovan text if desired.

#### Ledger Counting House

Handout:

`religion-naevra-record-of-a-stolen-name`

Why it fits:

This is the perfect placement for a stolen-name case file.

The Grave-Market likely buys and sells saint-bones, masks, name records, relic identities, and funerary claims. A record about a stolen name makes the market feel legally and spiritually obscene.

#### Auction Block of No Questions

Handout:

`religion-kharovan-magistrate-letter`

Why it fits:

This is a good place to begin doubting Kharovan's institutions.

The private magistrate letter suggests that Kharovan's priests may prefer order over justice. In a market where no questions are asked, that criticism lands naturally.

Optional alternative:

`religion-naevra-death-book-page`

Use this if you want a quieter, sadder contrast: one ordinary death record among a market that treats the dead as goods.


### Dungeon 5: The Silent Cathedral

This should be the main religious exposition dungeon.

It can contain one larger Naevra booklet and one Kharovan/Crown clue.

#### Bell-Ringer Vestry

Handout:

`religion-naevra-booklet`

Why it fits:

The larger Naevra booklet, **Questions for the Newly Gravebound**, belongs here.

The cathedral has bells, crypts, mourning, confession, and burial architecture. This booklet gives players a full understanding of why names and burial rites matter before the campaign pivots harder into crown corruption.

#### Noble Confession Hall

Handout:

`religion-kharovan-succession-trial-note`

Why it fits:

The Noble Confession Hall is a strong place for succession anxiety.

The note about heirs touching a relic connects confession, nobility, legitimacy, fear, and the Crown's ability to judge or corrupt rulers.

Optional alternative:

`religion-kharovan-organ-crypt-sheet`

Use this instead if you want to tie the haunted organ more strongly into command/name magic.


### Dungeon 6: The Crown Vault

This is where Kharovan doubt becomes explicit and the Hollow Below starts to press through.

#### Chapel of Shut Mouths

Handout:

`religion-kharovan-crown-pact-confession`

Why it fits:

This should be the turning-point handout.

The confession says the miracle came from obeying the crown, not from praying to Kharovan. In a chapel of shut mouths, that is exactly the sort of truth someone would hide.

This is where players should start wondering whether Kharovan is false, absent, dead, or being impersonated by the Crown.

#### Crown-Hollow Door

Handout:

`religion-hollow-depth-mask-warning`

Why it fits:

This is the first explicit Hollow Below warning.

The party is close enough to the Crown Vault that divine answers, crown commands, and underground whispers are beginning to overlap. The warning teaches them: if something answers too quickly underground, be suspicious.


### Dungeon 7: The King Beneath

This is the final religious reveal space. It should be almost entirely Kharovan/Empty Crown/Hollow.

#### Broken Royal Altar

Handout:

`religion-kharovan-booklet`

Why it fits:

The larger Kharovan booklet, **The Crown Beneath: A Loyal Defense**, belongs at the final royal shrine.

At this point it should read as tragic propaganda. The words about duty, law, command, and rightful rule are not silly or evil. They are noble ideas placed beside proof that command can outlive justice.

#### Buried Throne

Handout:

`religion-kharovan-empty-crown-fragment`

Why it fits:

This should be the final truth-fragment.

The line "There is no king beneath the crown" belongs at the Buried Throne. It should feel like the campaign's theological blade finally going in.

Optional final addition:

`religion-hollow-final-stair-note`

Use this only if you want a stronger Hollow ending. It suggests that whatever speaks at the bottom may use familiar divine voices.


## Minimal Version

If you want only one religion handout per dungeon, use this:

1. Dungeon 1: `religion-naevra-grave-candle-instruction`
2. Dungeon 2: `religion-kharovan-buried-crown-litany`
3. Dungeon 3: `religion-naevra-nameless-warning`
4. Dungeon 4: `religion-naevra-record-of-a-stolen-name`
5. Dungeon 5: `religion-naevra-booklet`
6. Dungeon 6: `religion-kharovan-crown-pact-confession`
7. Dungeon 7: `religion-kharovan-empty-crown-fragment`

This keeps the campaign extremely focused:

- Naevra teaches what should have happened to the dead.
- Kharovan teaches what command claimed over the dead.
- The Empty Crown reveals what went wrong.


## Heavier Version

If you want two handouts per dungeon, use the main plan above.

Total:

- 14 handouts across seven dungeons.
- 7 Kharovan handouts.
- 5 Naevra handouts.
- 2 Hollow Below handouts.

This is still focused and campaign-appropriate.


## What Not To Place Here

Avoid these unless a specific room is rewritten to support them:

- Lioran handouts
- Maera handouts
- Hadrin handouts
- Orund handouts
- Thessa handouts
- Crucible handouts

Reason:

They are good lore, but Barrow Crown is not their campaign.

These faiths will feel much stronger when placed in arcs built around roads, hearths, mines, wilderness pacts, elemental shrines, or light against undeath.
